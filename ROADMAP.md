# FIVE — Roadmap

How FIVE becomes a deeper teacher **without changing the central mechanic**: you always have five songs, progress is song-specific, and everything exists to help you understand, improve, choose or own those five.

Each step below is judged by one question: *does it help one of YOUR FIVE SONGS?*

## Now (this build)

- Choose five, sing them, real listening, first profile, YOUR FIVE SONGS home.
- Sing again → compared with your best take of that song, with noise thresholds.
- Coach memory that accumulates; practice cut from the song; blind listening.
- Audition a song into a slot; history kept; coach suggestions for when a song has taught what it can, or your five all test the same thing.
- The Sixth: readiness from evidence, a hypothesis with predictions, judged against the take.
- Optional AI rewrite of the coach's words (text and numbers only).

## Next: understand the voice better

1. **Validate on real singers.** Collect consented takes from a range of amateur voices and recalibrate the thresholds: what counts as “in tune”, “steady”, “better than before”. Report each measurement's take-to-take noise per singer.
2. **Phrase-level coaching.** Let the coach point at a line, not just a note: “the second line is where you start drifting”, with the line playable on its own.
3. **Key recommendations.** Already computed from your steady range. Next, explain them (“the top note of Shenandoah sits two semitones above where you're steady”) and let you audition two keys side by side.
4. **Personalised warm-ups.** A minute before a song, built from that song's demands and your voice map: its widest leap, its longest note, its highest phrase, in your key.
5. **Breath.** Measure phrase lengths against where the song lets you breathe; teach one breath per line, for that song.
6. **Repeatability.** Show consistency across takes of a song (“you hit the entrance 4 times out of 5 now”), not just last-versus-best.

## Then: richer teaching, still from the songs

7. **Teacher-generated exercises.** Use the AI layer to propose new exercises from the evidence, always tied to a phrase of a song, always recorded and measured. Same structure as today's practice: a reason, a part of the song, and a judgement.
8. **Imitate, then stop imitating.** Sing a phrase like the record, then deliberately your own way; blind-compare; ask which felt like you. Audio can't decide this; your choice is the evidence.
9. **Timbre and resonance exploration.** Brightness, breathiness and onset quality as descriptions (“brighter here, airier there”), never as scores. Only once they're validated.
10. **Vocal health guardrails.** Notice patterns that suggest strain: high notes getting louder and less steady, sessions getting longer, notes sinking late in sessions. Suggest rest or a lower key. Never diagnose.
11. **Richer before/after.** Play first and best takes of the same line back to back; show the two pitch lines together.

## Five-slot intelligence

12. **Song recommendations.** Find songs that fill what your five don't test, and that fit your voice map. Bigger catalogue, same rule: a candidate must be auditioned.
13. **Bring your own song.** Automatic melody extraction from your own best take (or from a licensed reference) so any song can be one of your five. Until a melody is confirmed, the coach measures only what needs no reference: steadiness, range, breath, and consistency between your takes.
14. **Candidate-song auditions with prediction.** Before you audition, the coach predicts how it will go (as it does for the Sixth), so every audition also tests the coach.

## Graduation and beyond

15. **Graduation criteria, refined.** Today: three takes of each song, three measurably better, four in tune, a known steady range. Later: consistency, interpretation choices, and the singer's own judgement, weighed together.
16. **Long-term voice profile.** Range and steadiness over months, with your five as the anchor.
17. **Multiple completed Fives.** After graduation, start a new five. Keep the old one as an archive: the songs that prove where you were.
18. **Archive of previous Fives.** Your first five (belief), every five since, and the five you graduated with (proof).

## What we will not build

- A global score, leaderboard or streak.
- A lesson catalogue unrelated to your songs.
- Feedback the measurements can't support.
